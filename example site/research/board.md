# Wildflower Collective — Design Research Board

**Swept:** August 25, 2026 (fresh for this project) · Browser captures + verified URLs
**Lanes:** Category (therapy/mental-health) · Aspirational (outside-category)

---

## Category lane — what the market's best actually do

### C1. Two Chairs — twochairs.com *(browser capture, 8/25/26)*
Saturated marigold hero — no sage, no beige — with a serif headline, a proof stat
("1 million+ sessions and counting") as the kicker, and a speed promise in the subhead
("appointments in one week or less"). Persistent "Book appointment" in the nav; dual CTA
in the hero.
**Steal the feel:** color courage in mental health works; logistics (speed, fit) sold in
the first 50 words; booking never more than one glance away.

### C2. Octave — findoctave.com *(browser capture, 8/25/26)*
Warm peach-to-cream gradient ground, tiny letterspaced kicker ("THERAPY"), display serif
headline, then a single subhead that answers three objections in one line:
"Insurance-covered, evidence-based therapy with visible milestones — start in days."
Stat strip immediately below the fold.
**Steal the feel:** the one-line objection-killer subhead formula (insurance + method +
speed); warmth achieved through color temperature rather than photography.

### C3. Alma — helloalma.com *(verified URL)*
Insurance is the first thing the site talks about; "find a therapist who takes your
insurance" is the product. Warm neutrals with soft illustration accents, friendly serif.
**Steal the feel:** put CareFirst BCBS at hero level — in this market, in-network status
is a headline fact, not a footnote.

### C4. Healing Roots Therapy — via Azuro Digital's 2026 therapist-site roundup
(azurodigital.com/therapist-website-examples). Group practice praised for solving
team-choice paralysis: filterable team, per-therapist specialties, "not sure who to pick?"
fallback path.
**Steal the feel:** scaled to three clinicians — a "meet the team" row with specialty
tags and a "not sure? we'll match you" consult CTA beneath it.

### C5. Psychology Today profile pages — psychologytoday.com *(UX benchmark)*
The de-facto competitor for every local therapy search. Converts with: photo, fee,
insurance, specialties, availability — all visible without scrolling.
**Steal the feel:** the site must answer a PT profile's five questions faster than the
profile does, or the visitor bounces back to the directory.

### C6. Local field (Mid-Shore practice sites) — espsmd.com and Easton-area template sites
Institutional directories or SquareSpace templates: stock docks, sage gradients, "A
Peaceful Place to Heal." Uniformly muted, uniformly anonymous.
**Anti-reference:** this is the camouflage the brand must not wear. The beige field is
what a wildflower meadow differentiates against.

## Aspirational lane — outside the category

### A1. Flamingo Estate — flamingoestate.com *(browser capture, 8/25/26)*
Garden-luxe commerce. Painterly texture, saturated botanical color, condensed serif
wordmark, editorial confidence. The brand lives inside a *world* (the garden) and every
page extends that world.
**Steal the feel:** treat the wildflower meadow as a place the whole site inhabits — not
a logo motif repeated, but a world the visitor walks into. Painterly ≠ crafty; executed
with discipline it reads premium.

### A2. Fishwife — eatfishwife.com *(browser capture, 8/25/26)*
Illustration-led CPG: chunky bordered panels, sky blue against tomato red, script + slab
pairing. Illustration carries the entire brand identity; photography is secondary.
**Steal the feel:** the confidence of letting illustration lead. **Caution:** its volume
is CPG-loud; a therapy audience needs the same conviction at half the decibels.

### A3. Anna Farba — annafarba.com *(browser capture, 8/25/26)*
Botanical watercolor illustrator. Her *artwork* is superb; her *site* fades watercolor to
a ~10%-opacity backdrop behind centered serif text — the default move for watercolor
brands, and it reads generic.
**Convention to break:** never fade the meadow into wallpaper. Watercolor lives at full
saturation in contained, intentional moments (a hero meadow band, a painted divider, a
flower assigned per therapist) — cream space stays clean around it.

### A4. Diaspora Co. — diasporaco.com *(verified URL)*
Single-origin spice company: vivid turmeric/chili/cardamom color blocking, editorial serif,
joy with rigor. Proof that saturated color + serious typography = warmth *and* authority
simultaneously.
**Steal the feel:** hue-blocked sections — each content zone owns one brand hue at low
tint with its deep partner for text, so the page moves through the palette like a garden
walk instead of one endless cream scroll.

### A5. Wildflower cases — wildflowercases.com *(verified URL; shares the name, different market)*
Gen-Z accessory brand built on hand-drawn florals and saturated color. Relevant only as
proof the "wildflower" visual space skews young without losing femininity — useful for
the teen half of the audience.
**Steal the feel:** florals can feel current, not grandmotherly; it's a saturation and
type-attitude question.

## Convention map — honor / break

| # | Category convention | Call | Why |
|---|--------------------|------|-----|
| 1 | Sage-and-beige muted palette | **BREAK** | The logo already owns six saturated hues; every local competitor wears beige. Color *is* the differentiation (C1, C6, A4). |
| 2 | Calm, low-density layout, generous whitespace | **HONOR** | Anxious, decision-fatigued visitors need low cognitive load. Cream stays the ground; color is applied, not ambient (A3 caution). |
| 3 | Serif display + humanist sans body | **HONOR** | Credibility + warmth is the right read for a 35–55 parent; execution stays fresh via scale and color, not novelty fonts (C2). |
| 4 | Stock nature/office hero photo | **BREAK** | Replace with brand-owned watercolor meadow + real team photography. No docks, no Adirondack chairs (C6, A1). |
| 5 | Booking CTA above the fold; phone in header | **HONOR** | This is the entire job of the site. Free 15-min consult is the one action; tel: link rides the header (C1, C5). |
| 6 | Fees and insurance buried or omitted | **BREAK** | Radical transparency: CareFirst at hero level, fees on a dedicated page. Beat the Psychology Today profile at its own game (C3, C5). |
| 7 | Team grid with matching help (group practices) | **HONOR** | Three faces with specialty tags + "not sure? we'll match you" (C4). |
| 8 | First-person solo-practice voice | **BREAK** | The current site's "I" hides two bookable therapists. Collective "we" for the practice; each clinician keeps her own voice on her bio. |
| 9 | Minimal/no motion | **HONOR, upgraded** | Soft, weighted reveals (petal-drift subtlety), full `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback. Motion says "cared for," never "look at me." |
| 10 | Credential badge walls | **HONOR selectively** | CareFirst, IACP, LCSW-C/LGPC/LGSW as designed trust markers — three meaningful marks, not twelve directory badges. |
