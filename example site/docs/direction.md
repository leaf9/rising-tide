# Wildflower Collective — Direction Synthesis

**Date:** August 25, 2026 · Follows `docs/discovery.md` + `research/board.md`

## Brand foundation (existing, mandated)

The logo is the brand's one finished artifact and it is genuinely good: hand-painted
watercolor wildflowers — poppy coral, meadow pink, lavender, cornflower, marigold, leaf
green — rising from the bottom edge of a warm cream circle, under a wide-tracked serif
wordmark in warm charcoal (#403830). Extracted brand facts:

- **Values (evidenced, not invented):** collaborative (it's in the name and the
  supervision model); growth-oriented (early-career therapists cultivated toward
  independence — literally a practice that grows practitioners); accessible (CareFirst,
  sliding scale, pro bono); grounded in place (Easton, the Eastern Shore).
- **Personality:** warm, credentialed, unfussy, quietly joyful. A meadow, not a spa.
- **Hard constraints:** the logo as-is; its cream ground and charcoal ink; the watercolor
  florals as the identity's visual DNA. Palette below is *extracted from the logo pixels*
  (see styleguide for measured values and contrast math).

**The central design question:** every competitor within 50 miles wears sage-beige
camouflage. The logo already owns six saturated hues. Does the site inherit the meadow's
color (and how loudly), or earn trust through restraint? The three concepts answer this
differently.

---

## Concept 1 — «Meadow in Broad Daylight» ★ recommended

**Argument.** The researching mom arrives anxious and decision-fatigued, comparing three
beige template sites and a Psychology Today profile. Trust needs calm; *choice* needs
difference. This concept keeps the category's calm chassis — cream ground, generous space,
serif authority (convention map: honor 2, 3, 5) — and breaks exactly where the logo gives
permission: color (break 1) and imagery (break 4). The watercolor meadow is used the way
Flamingo Estate uses its garden (A1): as a world, not a wallpaper. Sections are
hue-blocked like Diaspora's spice walk (A4) — each zone owns one flower's tint with its
deep partner for text — so the page reads as a garden walk, while conversion mechanics run
Two Chairs-style (C1): consult CTA in the hero, CareFirst named in the subhead, three
faces above the fold. Anna Farba's site (A3) is the cautionary opposite: watercolor faded
to backdrop reads generic — here the meadow stays at full saturation in *contained bands*
and the cream stays clean around it.

**Ingredients.**
- **Type attitude:** Fraunces (soft-wonk display serif, real optical sizes) for headlines
  — warm authority with a hand-made edge that rhymes with watercolor; Karla for body/UI —
  plainspoken, humanist, slightly grid-bent. Kicker style: small caps, wide tracking,
  deep-hue color.
- **Color logic:** cream ground `#f8f4ec`, charcoal-brown ink `#403830`; six bloom hues
  from the logo, each in a bloom (decorative) + deep (AA text/CTA) pair. Poppy-deep is the
  single CTA color sitewide. One hue per section as a 6–8% tint block.
- **Imagery treatment:** the meadow enters from page edges (a painted band growing along
  the hero's bottom, like the logo's own composition); real photography of the three
  clinicians, warm-graded, on cream; each therapist assigned a signature flower from the
  logo as her accent. Flat CSS stand-ins until Phase 5.
- **Layout attitude:** airy but load-bearing — every scroll answers an objection
  (who/where → insurance/fees → faces → how it works → consult). Soft-rounded cards
  (14px), hairline borders in ink-10%.
- **Motion character:** weighted fade-ups (450–600ms, ease-out-quart), petal-subtle
  parallax on the meadow band only, reduced-motion collapses to opacity.

**Citations:** A1, A4, A3 (as anti-pattern), C1, C3. **Proof:** `concepts/meadow-in-broad-daylight.html`

## Concept 2 — «Easton Light»

**Argument.** The counter-thesis: for a 35–55 Talbot County parent, the deepest trust
signal isn't difference — it's *place*. This concept honors the category's restraint
(honor 1, 2, 9) and differentiates through geographic specificity instead of chroma
(break 4 executed photographically): real Eastern Shore light — tidewater, marsh grass,
morning haze on the Choptank — replacing the stock docks of the local field (C6). The
palette narrows the logo to its quietest axis (cornflower, leaf, cream, charcoal) with
poppy held back as the lone CTA signal — Octave's temperature-not-decoration warmth (C2).
The wildflowers appear only where the brand signs its name: logo, favicon, a single
painted rule above the footer. Typography does the emotional work: a light editorial
serif at large sizes, Psychology-Today-beating logistics presented like a well-set menu
(C5). Risk, stated honestly: it borders the category's beige camouflage; its defense is
photographic specificity — no stock, ever.

**Ingredients.**
- **Type attitude:** Newsreader display (light, editorial, slightly literary) + Inter
  body/UI. Big quiet headlines, small precise labels.
- **Color logic:** cream + charcoal chassis; cornflower-deep `#3f6491` as the working
  accent; leaf for confirmation states; poppy-deep reserved exclusively for the consult
  CTA — one warm spark on a cool page.
- **Imagery treatment:** photography-led — Shore landscapes at dawn, the Easton office,
  unposed clinician portraits in natural light; watercolor restricted to brand signature
  moments.
- **Layout attitude:** editorial column grid, full-bleed photo bands alternating with
  narrow text measures; logistics tables set like fine print done proudly.
- **Motion character:** near-still; slow crossfades (800ms), no parallax.

**Citations:** C2, C5, C6 (anti-reference), A3 (restraint lesson inverted). **Proof:** `concepts/easton-light.html`

## Concept 3 — «The Kitchen Table»

**Argument.** Half the real audience is a 12–17-year-old who gets handed the phone:
"look at this therapist, what do you think?" If the teen recoils, the parent keeps
scrolling. This concept aims at the *family conversation* rather than the parent alone —
the warmth of the kitchen table where hard things get talked about. It trades the
watercolor's romance for its *linework*: single-weight ink drawings of stems and blooms
(the logo's skeleton, not its paint), chunky rounded type, and the logo's hues flattened
to matte, Fishwife's conviction at therapy volume (A2, with its stated caution applied).
Wildflower Cases (A5) proves florals can read current to a teen. Voice turns
conversational ("What's on your mind?" over "Comprehensive clinical services"), honoring
the category's warmth conventions while breaking its solemnity — the practice's own DBT
framing ("skills for real life") fits a practical, unfussy visual language. Risk: leans
young for the divorce/collaborative-professional referral audience; co-parenting pages
would carry the sober end of the spectrum.

**Ingredients.**
- **Type attitude:** Bricolage Grotesque (chunky, warm, a little irregular) for display +
  Nunito Sans body. Sentence-case headlines, big and friendly.
- **Color logic:** warm paper `#faf6ee`; matte flattenings of the logo hues — butter,
  denim, terracotta, moss — used as card fills; ink `#403830` everywhere else; poppy-deep
  CTA.
- **Imagery treatment:** hand-drawn single-weight ink botanicals (from the logo's stem
  structures) as spot illustrations and margin doodles; casual, honest team snapshots —
  no studio portraits.
- **Layout attitude:** stacked rounded cards with visible 1.5px ink borders, sticker-like
  specialty tags, generous tap targets; mobile-first to the point of designing the phone
  view first.
- **Motion character:** small springy micro-interactions on cards/tags (120–200ms),
  scroll reveals minimal.

**Citations:** A2, A5, C4, C1. **Proof:** `concepts/the-kitchen-table.html`

## Decision

**«Easton Light» — selected by Ben, August 25, 2026.** The style guide
(`styleguide/index.html` v2.0) is built on this direction; the v1.0 Meadow guide is
superseded. The Kitchen Table still donates its specialty-tag and conversational-microcopy
ideas where they fit Easton Light's register.

## Original Leaf9 recommendation (for the record — not selected)

**«Meadow in Broad Daylight».** It is the only concept where the mandated brand asset,
the competitive gap, and the conversion job all point the same direction: the logo
supplies the palette, the beige local field supplies the contrast, and the calm-chassis +
color-courage combination serves both the anxious parent and the teen veto. Easton Light
is the safe fallback if the client flinches at color; The Kitchen Table donates its
specialty-tag stickers and conversational CTA microcopy to whichever concept wins.
