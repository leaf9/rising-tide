01 · Color

A cool page, a lavender signal

The palette narrows the logo to its quietest axis — tidewater blue and marsh green on cool cream — and gives the consult button the meadow's lavender, deepened for contrast: calm, unmistakable, never alarming (Ben's call: no red CTAs). The full watercolor palette survives as signature hues inside artwork. All working colors are WCAG AA-verified on cream (ratios shown on each chip).

02 · Typography

Newsreader × Inter

Newsreader at large optical sizes and light weights gives literary calm — trust through composure, not decoration. Inter carries body and UI without personality intruding. No italic color accents in headlines: the restraint is the style.

Kicker · Inter 600 caps · .26em · 11px · tide

uncertain seasons.

H2 · Section voice, Newsreader 300

H3 · Card and block titles, Newsreader 400

Body — Inter 340 at 16.5px/1.68, ink on cream, max measure 60ch. Comfortable for an anxious reader on a phone at 9pm: no justified text, nothing lighter than 340 below 18px, generous paragraph spacing.

Small — 14px ink-soft for metadata, captions, credentials. Fees and logistics are set at body size in the menu table — transparency is a feature, not fine print.

  • Scale: H1 clamp(40–64px) · H2 clamp(30–42px) · H3 clamp(21–26px) · body 16.5px · small 14px · kicker 11px.
  • Weights: Newsreader 250 (H1), 300 (H2), 400 (H3), optical sizing on; Inter 340/500/600 only.
  • Type and art never overlap: headlines sit beside or above artwork on clean cream, never on top of it. The composition is editorial adjacency, magazine-style.
  • Wordmark lockup (Ben, Aug 25, 2026): the simplified circular mark (assets/brand/wildflower-mark.png — three signature flowers: poppy, lavender, cornflower) at 40–46px, with "WILDFLOWER" stacked above "COLLECTIVE" to its right — Newsreader 400, ~14.5px, letterspaced .22em. The original full logo is for large-format and social use only; it does not scale below ~150px.
03 · UI Components

Sharp corners, quiet confidence

Near-square 2px radius throughout — editorial, not app-like. Buttons are rectangles; the primary appears once per viewport-height and always says the same thing. Each therapist card carries her signature flower — a watercolor study from the logo's meadow (Carolyn: poppy · Amanda: lavender · Logan: cornflower) — and the fee table is set like a menu a good restaurant is proud of. The card's art slot accepts a portrait later with zero layout shift if the client ever wants one.

04 · Artwork & texture

The meadow, painted — no photography

The site's entire art program is generated watercolor botanical illustration in the logo's own palette and brushwork — the brand's one visual asset, extended into a family. The discipline that keeps it editorial rather than crafty: artwork is contained and studied (botanical-plate composure, not riotous bouquets), painted at full saturation, always on clean cream, and type never sits on top of it. Texture comes from the paint itself — brush edges, pigment blooms, paper grain — never from CSS filters over content.

Hero & band art — sparse meadow compositions, flowers rising from the bottom edge (the logo's own gesture), generous cream between stems. Wide formats (21:9, 24:5); flat stand-in shown at correct proportions.
Signature flower · 1:1 study
Signature flowers & spots — single-stem botanical studies: one per therapist (poppy · lavender · cornflower), plus small spot stems for section punctuation. Centered low, negative space above, like plates in a field guide.

05 · Motion

Near-still

Movement is weather, not choreography: slow crossfades, nothing slides, nothing parallaxes. A page that holds still reads as a practice that holds steady. Click the box to feel the fade.

click to replay fade
  • Reveal: opacity 0→1 only · 800ms · cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1) · stagger siblings 120ms. No translateY.
  • Hover (Ben, Aug 25, 2026): buttons darken + lift 1px with a soft shadow, 200ms; quiet links deepen and thicken their underline; cards gain a soft shadow. No scaling, nothing larger than a 1px lift.
  • Never: parallax, bounces, carousels, scroll-jacking, animated headlines.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: everything instant — already wired into this page.
06 · Voice & UX copy

Composed, local, plain

The practice speaks as "we" (three therapists, one collective); each clinician keeps "I" on her own bio. The register is a steady neighbor who happens to be excellent at her job — write to the parent at 9pm and the teen reading over her shoulder, both at once.

Do

  • "Request a free consult" — the one CTA phrase, everywhere.
  • Name the place: "in Easton," "on the Shore" — locality is trust.
  • Answer money questions plainly: "Sessions are $175. We're in-network with CareFirst BCBS, and sliding scale is available."
  • Plain clinical translation: "DBT — practical skills for big feelings."
  • Near every form: "Not for emergencies — if you're in crisis, call or text 988."

Don't

  • No "journey," "safe space," "holistic healing" boilerplate — every local competitor already says it.
  • No first-person "I" outside individual bios.
  • No urgency tricks or "limited spots!" — wrong category, wrong ethics.
  • No unexplained acronyms (DBT/CBT always get their plain-English shadow).
  • No collecting sensitive details in forms — name + contact + optional "what brings you here."
